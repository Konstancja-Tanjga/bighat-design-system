# List View

A selectable master list driving a detail pane.

<Canvas of={ListViewStories.MasterDetail} />

## Why it is a listbox

`List` renders records. `ListView` renders a **choice** — the selected row
determines what the pane beside it shows. That is a listbox, and the listbox
pattern is what makes a long list usable without a mouse: one tab stop, arrows
to move, Home and End to jump, type-ahead to find.

## Do and don't

### One tab stop, not one per row

<Heuristic name="Flexibility and efficiency of use">
  Type-ahead is the accelerator that makes a long list bearable. It costs nothing to the user who
  never discovers it, and it is the difference between usable and unusable for the one who does.
</Heuristic>

### Selection is never colour alone

The selected row gets a tinted background **and** a leading bar. On a
monochrome display, in high contrast, or for anyone who cannot separate two
close greys, the bar is the cue that survives.

### Multi-select is deliberately absent

### Empty gets a real explanation

<Canvas of={ListViewStories.Empty} />

`empty` takes a line of text for the trivial case. Anything with a cause and a
next step — a failed load, a filter that matched nothing — is `StateBlock`,
which is the component that already answers "what happened and what now".

## Accessibility

- `role="listbox"` with a required `ariaLabel`, `role="option"` rows with
  `aria-selected`.
- Focus stays on the container; the current row is tracked with
  `aria-activedescendant`.
- Arrow keys wrap and skip disabled rows; Home and End jump to the ends;
  printable characters start a type-ahead that resets after 800ms.
- Scrolling the selection into view is feature-detected, so keyboard selection
  still works where no layout engine exists.
