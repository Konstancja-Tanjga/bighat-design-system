# Article

A long read: text at a reading measure, a margin beside it for figures and
asides, and a table of contents that stays in view where there is room.

<Canvas of={ArticleStories.Default} />

## When to use it

| Use `Article` for                                   | Use something else for                                              |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A lesson, a guide, a help page - text read in order | An application screen of lists, tables or boards - `AppShell` alone |
| Many pages that are the same page, such as a course | A paragraph in a card or panel - plain text, or a `Card`            |

`Article` lays out; it does not style the text inside it. Headings, lists,
tables and quotes in the body are the product's, because long-form typography
is a product decision, and so are the heading ids the table of contents links
to. What the system owns is the part that is hard to copy correctly: the
measure, the margin, and when each of them gives way.

## Three shapes, by its own width

The article measures itself, not the window, because inside `AppShell` a
sidebar takes part of the screen.

- **Wide** (about 63rem and up): text, margin, and the table of contents,
  which stays in view while the reader scrolls.
- **Medium** (about 50rem and up): text and margin. The table of contents folds
  into a disclosure under the header, because a sticky list in the margin would
  sit on top of the notes that share that column.
- **Narrow:** one column. Every margin note returns to the text.

## The margin

<Guidance>
  <Do reason="A note placed right before its paragraph floats into the margin level with the top of that paragraph, and on a narrow screen sits just above it, the way a figure sits above the text that discusses it.">
    <p style={{ margin: 0 }}>
      `&lt;ArticleMargin&gt;photo&lt;/ArticleMargin&gt;` then the paragraph about the photo.
    </p>
  </Do>
  <Dont reason="Placed after its paragraph, the note floats beside the next one, and the photo of a kestrel ends up next to the text about falcons.">
    <p style={{ margin: 0 }}>The paragraph, then `&lt;ArticleMargin&gt;` for it.</p>
  </Dont>
</Guidance>

A margin note is a `div`, not an `aside`: an article with six notes would
otherwise add six "complementary" regions to a screen reader's list of
landmarks. Put a heading or an `aria-label` inside the note if it needs a name.
How a product marks content for the margin - a Markdown convention, a
component - is the product's decision.

## Props

| prop       | values            | default        | meaning                                                                         |
| ---------- | ----------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `title`    | slot              | -              | The `h1`, and the name of the article landmark.                                 |
| `eyebrow`  | slot              | -              | Above the title: `Breadcrumbs`, or the place in a sequence.                     |
| `meta`     | slot              | -              | Under the title: "Lesson 1 of 5 · 12 minutes".                                  |
| `lead`     | slot              | -              | One or two sentences.                                                           |
| `toc`      | `{ id, label }[]` | -              | Headings in the body. Omit, or pass none, for an article too short to need one. |
| `tocLabel` | string            | `On this page` | Names the table of contents navigation.                                         |
| `footer`   | slot              | -              | After the body: a completion control, the next article.                         |

## Accessibility

- **Role** - `article`, named by the title through `aria-labelledby`.
- **Table of contents** - a `nav` named by `tocLabel`. The rail and the folded
  copy are both in the markup; CSS shows one, and `display: none` removes the
  other from the accessibility tree, so a reader never meets two.
- **Where the reader is** - the entry for the section in view carries
  `aria-current="location"`, and is marked by weight and a leading line, not
  colour alone.
- **Focus** - links move to their heading through the browser's own fragment
  navigation; the component moves no focus. Give headings `scroll-margin-top`
  if a sticky header would cover them.
