# NavList

The grouped list inside a side panel — pinned and recent conversations, saved
views, a folder tree one level deep.

<Canvas of={NavListStories.Default} />

## When to use it

| Use `NavList` for                                             | Use something else for                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Navigating to a place: a conversation, a saved view, a folder | Choosing a record that fills a detail pane — use `ListView` |
| A short, named group of destinations in a panel               | Rows with columns, sorting or a caption — use `Table`       |

The boundary is what the click _means_. A `NavList` item takes you somewhere;
a `ListView` row answers a question the pane beside it is asking. That is why
`ListView` is a listbox with one tab stop and arrow keys, and this is a plain
list of buttons: navigation is not a selection, and pretending otherwise gives
the user a control that announces "selected" for something they have left.

Nesting is one level, and the level is a `NavGroup`. A tree that needs two
levels needs expand and collapse, roving focus and `aria-expanded` — that is a
different component, and building it out of nested `NavGroup`s produces
something that looks like a tree and answers to none of its keyboard contract.

## Every item is a button

An item is a `<button type="button">`, not a `<div>` with a click handler and
not an `<a>` unless the consumer wraps one. It is in the tab order, it takes
Enter and Space for free, and it announces as an activatable thing.

The active item carries `aria-current="true"` as well as its highlight. On a
monochrome display, or for anyone who cannot separate two close greys, the
attribute is the cue that survives — and it is the same rule the rest of the
system follows: state is never colour alone.

## The trailing slot is not part of the name

<Canvas of={NavListStories.WithMeta} />

`meta` takes a `Badge` or a count and is kept out of the accessible name on
purpose. "Overdue, 3" would read as though the destination were called that;
the destination is called "Overdue", and the 3 is a fact about it that changes
every hour. Anything the user must hear to understand the item belongs in
`label` or `subline`.

## Long content wraps, it does not disappear

<Canvas of={NavListStories.LongContent} />

A panel is narrow and the names in it come from real customers, so the label
wraps to as many lines as it needs and the subline truncates to one. Truncating
the label would hide the only thing distinguishing two items that share a
prefix — and in an ERP estate, a great many of them do.

## Accessibility

- **Role** — list (implicit, from `<ul>`), with `<li>` children and a button
  inside each one.
- **Labelling** — required in practice, not in types. `NavGroup` labels itself
  from its visible heading; a bare `NavList` takes `ariaLabel`. An unnamed list
  in a panel of three lists is three unnamed lists.
- **Focus** — one tab stop per item, in document order. Deliberate: a panel
  holds a handful of destinations, and the roving-tabindex pattern that saves
  keystrokes in a forty-row list costs discoverability here. If a list is long
  enough for that to hurt, it is a `ListView`.
- **Announcements** — none. Navigation is announced by what the page does next,
  and a live region that says "moved to Pinned" is a second, redundant voice.
